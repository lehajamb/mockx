MockX — a FIX-speaking mock exchange

Every order lives on two clocks.

One is simulated — a deterministic event queue that models network hops and engine processing as latency, without ever calling sleep(). The other is real — a monotonic wall-clock reading taken purely to see how long matching actually took to compute. MockX keeps the two scrupulously separate. This page walks through both subsystems using one order, ORD-1042, from submission to its last execution report.

Part one

The scheduler decides when.

LatencyScheduler is a discrete-event simulator: a min-heap of ScheduledEvent objects ordered by (timestamp, sequence). Nothing in MockX physically waits — instead, work is scheduled some number of simulated milliseconds in the future, and a simulated clock (current_time) is advanced to reveal it. The sequence counter is the tie-breaker: two events landing at the exact same simulated millisecond still fire in the order they were queued, which is how a passive fill's report always beats the aggressor's.

driving mode

external — nothing advances time on its own. A test harness or admin endpoint calls run_until() / advance_time() explicitly, so a whole trading day can be replayed faster than it takes to read this sentence.

realtime — a background task wakes every interval_ms and calls run_until(current + interval_ms), so the simulated clock tracks the wall clock closely enough to serve a live FIX session.

fail-stop, not silent

If a handler raises, or max_events_per_advance (100,000 by default) is exceeded in one advance, the scheduler flips to FAILED and records a SchedulerFailure dead-letter — event id, type, timestamp, exception. Every later call raises until the process is restarted. Nothing gets quietly dropped or retried into a loop.

Part two

The engine decides what.

At T+15, process_order() hands ORD-1042 to _match_aggressive_order(). Strict price-time priority: walk the best opposing price level, drain its resting orders FIFO, move to the next level only once the current one is exhausted, stop the instant the aggressor's quantity or limit price runs out. Every fill updates both orders, the book, and fires two execution reports — passive first, aggressor second — before the next fill is even computed.

The loop above stops the instant remaining_qty hits zero, so ORD-1042 never reaches level 100.30 and never needs _finalize_unfilled_remainder(). That function only matters when quantity is left over once the book runs out of eligible price — and it treats three cases differently:

Market or IOC

Remainder is canceled outright

Market orders never rest, and IOC means immediate-or-cancel. Whatever quantity didn't fill immediately is canceled and reported as such — no trace left in the book.

GTC, nothing filled

Rests, with one NEW report

A wholly unfilled limit order joins its price level and gets a single ExecutionType.NEW report acknowledging it's live.

GTC, partially filled

Rests silently

The TRADE/PARTIAL reports already sent during matching are the acknowledgement — no extra NEW report follows the fills it already got.
